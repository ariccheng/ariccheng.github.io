Ridgeline Boards

🏔️ Ridgeline Boards is a small workshop that builds two models of snowboard, the Trail board 🏂 and the Summit board 🎿. Every Monday the shop manager sets the week's production: how many of each model to build. Each board passes through two departments, fabrication and finishing, and each department has a fixed number of hours available that week. The shop's resort dealers also require at least two Summit boards for every Trail board. The question is which schedule earns the most gross profit.

Resource Trail (T) Summit (S) Available per week
Gross profit per board $50 $65 — maximize
Fabrication hours 3.5 4.0 84
Finishing hours 1.0 1.5 21
Dealer mix At least two Summit boards per Trail board S ≥ 2T

The three colours mark the three constraints, and the lines on every graph below carry the same colours.

The same problem in standard linear programming form:

Max 50T + 65S Gross profit for the week
s.t. 3.5T + 4S ≤ 84 Fabrication, 84 h
T + 1.5S ≤ 21 Finishing, 21 h
−2T + S ≥ 0 Dealer mix
T, S ≥ 0 No negative production
